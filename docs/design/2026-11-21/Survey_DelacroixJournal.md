## 1. The facts found

- **Two journals and a gap.** A first journal 1822–1824, a second 1847–1863. Between them the writing is suspended: of those twenty-three years only scattered letters, loose sheets, notebooks and the paintings remain.
- **Why he kept it, in his words** (quoted in the preface of the 1893 edition; date of the entry not found): « Il me semble que je suis encore le maître des jours que j'ai inscrits, quoiqu'ils soient passés ».
- **The notebooks of the second journal hold preparatory notes for other writings**, among them a Dictionnaire des Beaux-Arts, considered just after his election to the Académie.
- **A diversity of texts** in one document (the title of the Universalis section on it). Daily life, technique, reflection on art, music and letters side by side (general knowledge).
- **Three editions.** Flat and Piot, 1893–1895, after the manuscripts had passed through heirs. Joubin, 1931–1932. Hannoosh, 2009 (José Corti, two volumes), redone entirely from the original manuscripts and newly identified sources.
- **What the 2009 edition repaired:** hundreds of errors in the earlier text; notes that had been left out; forgotten annotations; texts newly found; and **the original order of the notes, which matters for his aesthetic project**. A reviewer: earlier editors « often suppressed the chaotic quality of the journal ».
- **What it kept apart:** material that was not originally in the diaries is separated from what was.
- **What was lost:** the 1863 diary no longer exists; it survives in several copies, whose differences the edition publishes.
- **What it added around the text:** short biographies of the hundreds of people mentioned, family genealogies, a bibliography, an index.
- The editor's own account is subtitled « archéologie et reconstitution d'un document » and is described as reading like a detective story.

## 2. What it teaches the sketchbook

| Lesson | From | In the hub |
|---|---|---|
| **A day written down stays yours; a day not written is gone.** Capture must cost nothing, or the journal stops — for twenty-three years. | The quote; the gap | The « right now » entry on the phone; a note taken with no signal is kept and sent later |
| **The order as written is part of the meaning.** Never tidy the stream into a better order. | The 2009 edition restoring the order | Entries are never rewritten or moved; the feed shows them as they came. Other orders (by work, by idea, by label) are **views**, never the record |
| **The disorder is the document.** Do not force a structure at the moment of writing. | Editors who suppressed « the chaotic quality » | No type picker at capture (Pem's ruling); labels come after, several allowed |
| **Keep what he wrote apart from what was added around it.** | The edition separating foreign material | Every item says where its text comes from: typed by Pem, dictated, read by a machine from handwriting, suggested by a machine. The hub has `provenance`, `verification` and `proposed_by`; the design must keep them visible, and a machine's words must never pass for Pem's |
| **Keep the original beside the copy.** A copy has variants; a lost original cannot be checked. | The 1863 diary known only by copies | The voice recording beside its transcript, the page scan beside its reading; a correction is added beside the original, never over it (`corrects_id`) |
| **The journal feeds the work of thought.** Notes become entries of a dictionary of ideas. | The Dictionnaire notes inside the notebooks | A moment becomes an idea and the idea keeps the link to its moment (Idées); the « process » view |
| **The people in it need a directory.** A reader a century later needs to know who is who. | The 2009 apparatus: hundreds of biographies, an index | A person mentioned in a moment is a link to the contact; the person's page shows the moments (backlinks). The index = search and labels |
| **Without a guide, the heirs do archaeology.** | Three editions and a reconstruction over 116 years | The stranger's guide and the archive export (survey 3): entries in their order, with their provenance, readable without the app |
| **Technique notes outlive the painter.** Restorers and scholars read them. (general knowledge) | — | The « materials and making » remark on a work |
